# Work documented on the Maniago page that isn't among the nine

You asked whether the page holds projects the Foundation's own page doesn't. It does. None of these
have an entry in `data.json`, and none appear in the earlier research pack built from
facebook.com/luigifootprints. Each is worth a decision: new project entry, folded into an existing
one, or left as history.

---

## The Elephant Den (TED) — youth enterprise grants
**March 2022 · Nairobi (SUSO headquarters)**

Maniago and the Foundation teamed up with SUSO to run a summit at which five groups of young people
from **Nakuru, Amboseli, Taita Taveta, Embu and Nairobi** presented commercial business ideas,
competing for a grant to start them. The groups camped at the SUSO headquarters. A panel of four —
Peter Karanja and Annabella Francescon for Maniago and the Foundation, Peter Moll and Poebe for SUSO
— heard the pitches and advised on turning them into real businesses.

The Foundation described it in its own words as "a new chapter where we will empower the youth into
the future with commercial enterprises for their future livelihoods."

**Now a project entry** (`the-elephant-den`). It didn't end at the pitch: in April 2022 the Foundation
handed **four acres near Embu town** to SUSO's chief executive Peter Moll and the Embu County
Director, to be set up as a tree nursery and a base for other agricultural work — "part of the TED
initiative to give the youth a commercial, yet environmentally friendly, venture."

## The causeway at the Amboseli Trust for Elephants centre
**October 2020 · Amboseli**

Visiting the Amboseli Trust for Elephants with Pat Awori, the Foundation met Cynthia Moss and Norah
Njiraini and handed over a cheque to help fund a much-needed causeway to the ATE Centre. Rising water
levels had reached the point where the access road was being cut off during the rains.

**Probably belongs under partner support** rather than as a standalone project, but it is a concrete,
funded piece of infrastructure and the ATE relationship matters (see below).

## Luigi the elephant
**Named May 2017; announced January 2021 · Amboseli**

A young bull born in Amboseli in May 2017 was named **Luigi** by the Amboseli Trust for Elephants, in
honour of the late Luigi Francescon's generosity to the organisation through the Foundation
established in his memory. He is the son of Lemlepo, of the LD family. The name is entered
permanently in ATE's research database.

The existing research pack flagged the identity of Luigi as an open question and resolved it from
LFF's own page; this post is independent confirmation, with the elephant's parentage and the exact
provenance of the name. **Story material for the About page rather than a project.**

## The Kibra Community Challenge
**September 2020 · Kibra, Nairobi**

With the SUSO team, the Foundation backed what was called a Community Challenge — "a resilient
community for a sustainable future." The team cleaned, painted and built pathways at the **Phoenix
Library** and **Three Bells Primary School**, and started a green space beside the library which the
young people named **Little Karura**, after the city's much larger Karura Forest.

**Folded into the COVID-19 entry** in the copy I've written, since it grew directly out of that
work — but it could stand alone if you want the shift from relief to rebuilding to be visible.

## The first Amboseli tree nursery
**May 2021 · Amboseli**

"We have a tree nursery planned and we have a location." **Dr Winnie Kiiru** hosted the first nursery
at her place in the Amboseli area, and **Jim Nyamu** agreed to help care for the seedlings. Four
trees were planted to inaugurate it.

**This is the seed of the Olchani Project**, and you've confirmed it. The earlier pack dated the
community nurseries to late 2023; this post puts the first one two and a half years earlier, with
named hosts. Note that there is still **no Olchani entry in `data.json`**, though Ubuntu Hay calls
itself "the second phase of the Olchani Project" — see item 5 of the verification file.

## Dignity Housing, earlier than the current record
**September 2021 · Nairobi National Park**

The Foundation took **Martin Meyer and potential donors** to inspect the Dignity Housing units built
for the rhino rangers inside Nairobi National Park. **Senior Warden Joseph Dadacha** briefed them on
the rangers' role, noting that through their determination, training and dedication there had been
**no rhino poaching in the park in over seven years**.

**Corrected.** The entry now opens where the project actually starts: March 2021, with Senior Warden
Joseph Dadacha's idea, the first two container homes built jointly by Maniago, the Foundation and
SUSO, the 25 rhino blocks and the ~US$250,000 programme — then April 2021 installation, the third and
fourth homes in August, and this September 2021 donor visit, before the 2025–26 Samburu chapter.
Full sourcing in the capture log.

## The ranger torches
**June 2022 · Nairobi National Park**

Twenty KWS rangers were celebrated in the presence of the Assistant Director of Nairobi National Park
and his deputy, and received twenty powerful rechargeable torches they had specifically requested for
their security operations.

**Folded into the ranger boots entry** as part of the pattern of asking rangers what they need.
