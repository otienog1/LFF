## Intro copy for the top of the page (optional)

At Luigi Footprints Foundation we work where people and wildlife share the same ground — the plains of Amboseli, the savanna of Nairobi National Park, and the rangelands of Samburu. Our work rests on a simple belief: when the people who live alongside wildlife are supported with dignity, wildlife has a future too. That belief takes different shapes — a ranger who finally has a safe place to sleep, a woman earning an income from a tree nursery she runs, a child who learns conservation with soil on their hands. These are the projects behind *Thriving People, Thriving Wildlife.*

# 1. Dignity Housing Project

**Location:** Amboseli · Nairobi National Park · Samburu National Reserve, Kenya

**Short description**
Rangers spend weeks at a time living where the wildlife they protect lives — and for years, many did so in tents and worn-out uniports. The Dignity Housing Project builds them proper homes: safe, permanent units with beds, a kitchen, a washroom, running water and power. It is the Foundation's flagship work, built on one idea — that people who guard the wild deserve to return to somewhere worthy of the job.

**The work**
Each Dignity House is a fully contained unit fitted with mounted beds, shelves, a kitchenette and a washroom, and then equipped with water tanks and solar power so rangers have running water, warm showers and electricity. The Foundation has built these homes across three landscapes. In Nairobi National Park, homes were completed for the Rhino Rangers who protect the park's rhino sanctuary — the fifth unit was launched there in July 2025 and a sixth handed over in October 2025. In Samburu National Reserve, the Foundation broke ground in November 2025 on a new cluster of eight units, positioned at a critical bridge crossing that had never had a permanent ranger base. Construction ran through the end of 2025, and the Samburu housing was officially opened in early 2026. Around the homes, the Foundation and rangers plant trees together.

**Impact**
Where rangers once slept in tents, they now have permanent housing with the basics of a dignified life. In Samburu, the eight-unit base means a security point that was hard to staff can finally be manned around the clock, letting rangers respond faster and protect both wildlife and the surrounding communities. The Foundation frames it plainly: when rangers are supported, they can do more — and the wild is safer for it.

**Project story / timeline**
The project grew out of the Foundation's earliest ranger work in Amboseli and Nairobi National Park, where the first Dignity Houses — including homes for Nairobi's Rhino Rangers — were built and handed over through 2024 and into 2025. In July 2025, at the launch of the fifth unit in Nairobi National Park, the Kenya Wildlife Service read a message of appreciation for the partnership between KWS, Maniago Safaris, Luigi Footprints Foundation and Stand Up Shout Out. That October, the sixth house was handed to the Rhino Rangers. The work then expanded to Samburu: after a partnership meeting by the Ewaso Nyiro River between the Foundation's Peter Karanja, Honorary Warden Annabella Francescon and Reserve Director Tom Lesarge, the Foundation announced a new chapter — *"from the plains of Amboseli, to the savanna of Nairobi, to the heart of Samburu."* Ground was broken in November 2025 with Director Tom Lesarge and contractor Benson Omolo; construction progressed through December (visited by Annabella Francescon, who donated the Samburu units); and the housing was officially opened in early 2026 by the Governor of Samburu County, H.E. Jonathan Lati Lelelit. On World Ranger Day in 2026, the Foundation returned to plant trees around the Samburu homes.

# 2. Olchani Project

**Location:** Amboseli ecosystem, Kenya

**Short description**
*Olchani* means "tree" in the Maa language, and that is where this project starts: with communities in Amboseli growing indigenous trees, not as a one-off planting day but as a way of life. Run with community members — especially women and youth — and funded in partnership with ifaw, Olchani turns restoration into livelihoods, and livelihoods into a reason to protect the land.

**The work**
The Foundation helped set up and support community-run tree nurseries across the Amboseli ecosystem — among them the Osoit, Ildepen and Tembo nurseries — where local women ("mamas") raise indigenous seedlings and earn a dignified income doing it. Seedlings from these nurseries are planted out into restoration sites, most notably the Orregie Enclosure inside Amboseli National Park, envisioned as a small forest of several thousand indigenous trees that helps keep wildlife corridors open. Beyond the nurseries, the project carries a culture of tree-growing into households, churches and schools, placing seedlings directly into families' hands. Community meetings (*barazas*) anchor the work in collective decision-making.

**Impact**
The trees do two jobs at once: they restore habitat and they restore livelihoods. Women running the nurseries earn income; youth grow both trees and a sense of purpose; and the Amboseli landscape gains indigenous cover in the places it is most needed. When ifaw marked its 25th anniversary of conservation work in East Africa, its team planted trees from these very community nurseries — and the Foundation's trees have featured in national moments including World Wildlife Day in Amboseli.

**Project story / timeline**
The nurseries began around late 2023, set up with SUSO youth at the KWS Amboseli headquarters and at Oldoinyo Oibor Primary School. Early 2024 saw the launch of the Osoit ("Intomonok Osoit") nursery, and community members marked National Tree Planting Day by planting hundreds of seedlings at the Orregie Enclosure. Through 2024 and 2025 the project deepened — more nurseries, tree-growing outreach into homes and schools, and a widening partnership with ifaw, whose leadership visited and planted alongside the community. The Foundation describes Olchani in its own words: *"restoration is more than planting trees — mamas leading community nurseries and earning dignified incomes, youth growing trees while growing their purpose, communities healing land while healing livelihoods."*

# 3. Ubuntu Hay

**Location:** Lemongo, near Amboseli, Kenya

**Short description**
Drought is the hardest season for communities who keep livestock alongside wildlife. Ubuntu Hay — the second phase of the Olchani work — is a community-owned hay bank that stores fodder in the good seasons so that livestock, livelihoods and grasslands are protected when the rains are late or fail.

**The work**
Community members harvest grass, bale it into hay and fill a community hay barn built with them and for them. The idea is foresight: keep nourishment in store for dry days, reduce pressure on fragile grasslands, and give families a buffer that protects both their animals and the rangelands wildlife depends on. Women, men and youth run it themselves, with support from ifaw.

**Impact**
Ubuntu Hay turns restoration into security. Stored fodder means livestock can be sustained through drought without stripping the grasslands, and the community carries the knowledge and ownership to keep doing it. The Foundation calls it *"climate resilience stored for tomorrow and economic empowerment created today."*

**Project story / timeline**
Ubuntu Hay launched in mid-2025 as Olchani's second phase, with the community carrying out its first harvest-and-baling operation to fill the hay bank later that year. The Foundation has profiled the community leaders behind it — including the hay bank's chairman, John — and shared progress updates as the barn filled and the first dry season tested the model.

# 4. Ubuntu Smiles

**Location:** Amboseli ecosystem, Kenya

**Short description**
Ubuntu Smiles is the Foundation's women's economic-empowerment work — helping community women earn real, dignified incomes through crafts and their work in the tree nurseries, and with it, a measure of financial independence.

**The work**
The programme supports women to earn from their skills — beadwork and crafts, and income from the community nurseries they run — and connects that income to a wider sense of dignity and possibility. It is delivered alongside the Olchani restoration work and supported by ifaw.

**Impact**
The women who have taken part describe it in their own words. One community leader, Anna Touwa, has spoken about the economic empowerment Ubuntu Smiles brought to her life and to other women in her community. The Foundation frames the ripple effect simply: empowering women restores livelihoods and strengthens whole communities — *"when communities flourish, wildlife flourish too."*

**Project story / timeline**
The women's craft and income work appears across the Foundation's story — from early "value-addition" sessions with talented Amboseli women, to beadwork, to the named Ubuntu Smiles programme profiled in 2025. It sits within the same community-and-conservation partnership with ifaw that underpins Olchani.

**Images:** see *04-recommended-images.md → Ubuntu Smiles.*

# 5. Outdoor Classroom Series

**Location:** Amboseli — community tree nurseries (Osoit, Ildepen), Kenya

**Short description**
The Outdoor Classroom Series takes conservation education out of the four walls of a classroom and into the nurseries and the soil — where children from local primary schools learn about restoration by doing it, guided by the community leaders who live this work every day.

**The work**
The Foundation brings children from nearby primary schools to its community-led tree nurseries for hands-on conservation sessions. Lessons are led not only by teachers but by community coexistence leaders — for example, Anna Tova leading a session at the Ildepen nursery, and Nickson Seleon leading one at the Osoit nursery — teaching children about indigenous trees, restoration and living alongside wildlife.

**Impact**
The aim is a generation that sees conservation as something they have experienced, not just been told about. The Foundation puts it this way: *"education is not just something you are taught, it is something you experience"* — and when children learn conservation through their own hands, and communities lead that learning, the impact runs deeper.

**Project story / timeline**
The series ran through the 2025–2026 period, with documented sessions including "Learning Beyond the Classroom" at the Ildepen nursery and "Learning by Living It" at the Osoit nursery. It sits within the Olchani programme and complements the Foundation's wider education work.

**Images:** see *04-recommended-images.md → Outdoor Classroom.*

# 6. Education & Scholarships

**Location:** Amboseli (Oldoinyo Primary) and beyond, Kenya

**Short description**
Education sits at the heart of the Foundation. Alongside conservation, it supports children and young people through scholarships, conservation education and the nurturing of their talents — a commitment the Foundation describes as keeping *Luigi's legacy* alive.

**The work**
The Foundation invests in education in three ways it has named itself: conservation education that teaches children the value of protecting their natural heritage; nurturing the talents and skills of young people; and scholarships that open access to school regardless of a family's circumstances. It has sponsored students directly — including three girls from Oldoinyo Primary School in Amboseli — and has taken part, with other donors, in festive-season support for children such as the Inzeku pupils in Kakamega.

**Impact**
The Foundation measures this less in fees paid than in confidence built. It has followed the three sponsored girls — Hope, Fantasia and Sarah — as they moved through school, and celebrates the young people it supports as the generation that will decide the future of coexistence.

**Project story / timeline**
Education has been part of the Foundation's message since its earliest posts in 2023. The direct sponsorship of the three Oldoinyo Primary girls is documented from early 2025, with updates on their progress, and the wider scholarship, mentorship and conservation-education work runs throughout.

**Images:** see *04-recommended-images.md → Education & Scholarships.*

# 7. Ranger Support & Recognition

**Location:** Samburu · Nairobi National Park · Loitokitok, Kenya

**Short description**
Beyond building homes, the Foundation stands with rangers through the year — celebrating their service, equipping them with better tools, and backing the technology and recognition that help them do a hard job well.

**The work**
The Foundation marks World Ranger Day with rangers in the field, not just online — riding along, planting trees together, and joining ceremonies that honour their service. It has strengthened rangers' tools, too: handing over camera and documentation equipment to Samburu National Reserve (donated by Jeff and Sara Mora on behalf of the Peter and Edith Mora Foundation), and supporting the EarthRanger wildlife-monitoring system at Samburu's monitoring centre, which lets rangers track wildlife movements across the wider ecosystem and respond faster when something is wrong.

**Impact**
The Foundation's stated approach is to support the whole ranger — their dignity, wellbeing, training and tools. Better documentation equipment builds a visual and educational record of Samburu's wildlife, habitats and community conservation; monitoring technology helps rangers protect a vast landscape more effectively; and recognition sustains the people doing the work.

**Project story / timeline**
Ranger support runs from the Foundation's earliest days through to the present, celebrated each World Ranger Day (2024, 2025 and 2026) and deepened over time — the Samburu camera handover and the EarthRanger monitoring support are among the most recent milestones, alongside town clean-ups and tree-planting in the lead-up to World Ranger Day.

**Images:** see *04-recommended-images.md → Ranger Support.*

# 8. Youth in Conservation & the SUSO Partnership

**Location:** Nairobi National Park · Amboseli · Loitokitok, Kenya

**Short description**
Much of the Foundation's community work runs through young "changemakers" — mobilised in partnership with Stand Up Shout Out (SUSO) to restore parks, clear invasive species, clean up towns, and grow trees, all while learning to lead.

**The work**
Together with SUSO and the Kenya Wildlife Service, the Foundation supports the *Adopt-A-Park* model of sustained youth involvement in national parks. Documented activities include Mazingira Day at Nairobi National Park, where more than a hundred young volunteers cleared invasive Parthenium weed from the grasslands; a town clean-up in Loitokitok in the run-up to World Ranger Day, supported with tools and refreshments; and ongoing youth tree-growing in which young people restore landscapes, learn practical conservation skills and mentor children coming up behind them.

**Impact**
The Foundation's point is that conservation lasts when young people own it. Youth clear invasive species that choke the grasslands, plant and tend trees, and pass the knowledge on to children — turning passion into practical, repeatable action, and building a generation of local stewards.

**Project story / timeline**
The youth-and-SUSO partnership threads through the Foundation's whole history, from early drought-relief mobilisations to the Adopt-A-Park work across several parks, Mazingira Day 2025 at Nairobi National Park, and the 2026 Loitokitok clean-up.

# 9. Nanare Art Challenge & Art for Change

**Location:** SUSO Mabingwa Youth Centre, Nairobi (participants from Kakamega), Kenya

**Short description**
Sometimes empowerment looks like a paintbrush. The Nanare Art Challenge was an immersive leadership-development experience — built around art — for thirty teenagers from Kakamega, designed to help them discover the confidence and possibility already within them.

**The work**
Run in partnership with Stand Up Shout Out at the SUSO Mabingwa Youth Centre, the Challenge placed paint, fabric and possibility in front of thirty young people, many of whom had never drawn before, and invited them to create. Over several days it grew into far more than an art competition — a journey of leadership, collaboration and self-discovery, led by SUSO's founder Peter Fredrick Moll with SUSO staff and youth leaders (SUSONIANS) as facilitators. It built on earlier art experiences the Foundation has hosted with visiting artists, including an art day with young people in Kibera.

**Impact**
By the end of the experience, the Foundation reports, all thirty participants said they felt more confident, more exposed to new possibility, and more like changemakers — quiet voices grown stronger, strangers turned teammates.

**Project story / timeline**
The Foundation documented the Challenge as a series in 2025 — from the first day at the SUSO Mabingwa Youth Centre through the leadership experience and the final artwork — framed as a story of what happens when young people are given space to imagine and see themselves differently. The broader "Art for Change" thread includes an earlier art experience with youth in Kibera, hosted with a visiting artist.

**Images:** see *04-recommended-images.md → Nanare Art Challenge.*

# 10. Living Safely With Wildlife (Human–Wildlife Coexistence Training)

**Location:** Amboseli · Nairobi National Park · Samburu, Kenya

**Short description**
For communities who live beside wildlife every day, coexistence is a practical skill. The Foundation supports "toolbox" trainings that equip community ambassadors — including women leaders — with the knowledge and tools to live safely alongside wildlife and reduce conflict.

**The work**
The Foundation co-sponsored a Human–Wildlife Coexistence toolbox training led by Save the Elephants, bringing together community representatives from the Amboseli and Nairobi National Park areas together with SUSO youth. In Samburu, its "Living Safely with Wildlife" training saw community women leaders from the SUSO Coexistence Villages step forward — asking questions, sharing lived experience, and learning new approaches and technologies for coexistence.

**Impact**
The Foundation's aim is to strengthen the people who hold coexistence together every day. Its account of the Samburu training is direct: *"you don't just train a woman — you strengthen a leader,"* one who carries that knowledge home to her family, her village and the next generation.

**Project story / timeline**
The Save the Elephants toolbox training was documented in 2025; the Samburu "When Women Stand Up" training in 2026. Both sit within the Foundation's wider *Room to Roam* commitment to coexistence.

**Images:** see *04-recommended-images.md → Coexistence Training.*

# 11. Drought Relief & Emergency Response

**Location:** Nairobi National Park · Amboseli, Kenya

**Short description**
Some of the Foundation's earliest work was emergency response — getting hay, fodder and water to wildlife and livestock through hard drought.

**The work**
With SUSO and Maniago Safaris, the Foundation delivered hay, lucerne and water by the truckload and bowser to sustain wildlife and communities in drought — at Nairobi National Park (which it describes as "the world's only park in a capital city") and across the Amboseli ecosystem, alongside supplementary feeding for wildlife.

**Impact**
The relief kept animals and livestock fed and watered through the worst of the dry seasons, and it laid the relationships — with SUSO, Maniago and the Kenya Wildlife Service — that the Foundation's later projects grew from.

**Project story / timeline**
Drought relief runs through the Foundation's founding period in 2022 and 2023, including missions in the Amboseli ecosystem in late 2022 and hay-and-water support at Nairobi National Park in early 2023.

**Images:** see *04-recommended-images.md → Drought Relief.*
